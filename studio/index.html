Will it help your PC? Look at one number.

Press Ctrl+Shift+Esc, open the Performance tab, click CPU. Bottom left, next to Threads and Handles, is Processes. Close everything else first and read it.

  • Over 150: yes. That is the machine most people are gaming on.
  • 110 to 150: yes, and the power plan, network and BIOS checklist are most of the gain.
  • Under 100 with nothing open: you have already done this by hand. Keep your money.

Instant

A power plan called OmniDx. Built for frames.

Windows' "Balanced" plan parks cores, drops the CPU to 5% between frames and lets the PCIe bus and USB ports doze — every one of those costs a frame time spike. The OmniDx plan is made from Ultimate Performance and then pushed further, and it is active the moment the script finishes. Undo puts your old plan back.

🧠

CPU 100 / 100

Minimum and maximum processor state both at 100%, boost mode aggressive, core parking off on every core, active cooling. The clock is up before the game asks for it.

🔌

No bus ever sleeps

PCIe link state power management off, USB selective suspend off, USB 3 link power off, disks never spin down. Your GPU and your mouse are never waking up from anything.

🛌

No sleep, no hibernate, on mains

Sleep, hibernate and hybrid sleep off while plugged in. On a laptop the battery side of the plan stays sensible — it is your battery. Desktops also lose the hiberfile and Fast Startup, so every boot is a clean one.

Network

Latency over throughput on the adapter you actually use.

Downloads are not made slower — the "disable autotuning" myth that halves your speed is left where it belongs. What changes is how fast a small packet leaves your PC and how often the adapter is allowed to nap.

📶

On the live adapter

  • Nagle's algorithm off: small packets go now, not after a 200 ms wait for company
  • Adapter power management off, so the link never drops to save a watt
  • Energy-efficient Ethernet, green Ethernet, interrupt moderation, ultra-low-power mode off — by whichever name your driver uses
  • Optionally, DNS to 1.1.1.1 with -Dns. Off by default; your router is usually fine
🧮

In the TCP stack

  • Autotuning stays normal — the one setting people break most
  • ECN and timestamps off, receive-side scaling on, CTCP congestion control
  • The 20% bandwidth reservation for Windows' own traffic set to zero
  • Every one of these is recorded and put back by undo

Below Windows

The best BIOS settings for your board, on paper.

Nothing on the PC can change your BIOS for you, and anything that claims to should frighten you. What the script can do is read your board, your BIOS version and your CPU, and write the checklist for exactly that — which key to press, which menu each setting is under, and what to set it to. It opens at the end, and it is the most valuable page in the report.

🧬

Written for your board

  • ASUS, MSI, Gigabyte, ASRock, Dell, HP, Lenovo, Acer — the right key and the right menu names for each
  • EXPO on AMD, XMP on Intel: your RAM runs at the slow default until you flip this, and it is the single biggest free gain on any PC
  • Above 4G Decoding and Resizable BAR, and the CSM setting they depend on — only when you boot UEFI
  • Secure Boot and TPM: on, with the exact page, because Windows 11 and VALORANT expect them
📝

And what not to do

  • Precision Boost Overdrive on AMD, power limits on Intel — never a voltage
  • C-states and HPET left alone, with the reason: the 2013 tweaks that hurt today
  • Fan curves that let a boost clock stay up
  • Onboard devices you do not use, each one an interrupt source gone
  • Do not flash the BIOS unless the release notes name your problem — the one thing here that cannot be undone from Windows

Restore point, a record of every change, undo in one line.

The fear

"It'll break something and I won't know what"

Tweak tools from a video with no record of what they touched.

A System Restore point first, every run

Named and dated. If anything at all feels wrong, Windows can put the whole system back to the minute before the script started. It refuses to go on without one unless you tell it to.

The fear

"How do I get it all back?"

Reinstall Windows, usually.

Undo is one file, and it is yours

Every change lands in C:\OmniDx\undo\changes-latest.json with what it was before, and undo.ps1 next to it replays them in reverse: services, tasks, registry, the power plan, the app files from their backups. No key needed, no internet needed.

The fear

"Those tools turn Defender off"

The usual "optimizer" lowers your security to raise a number.

Security is never lowered

Defender, the firewall, UAC, SmartScreen, Secure Boot and Windows Update are not touched. The one performance-for-security trade that exists — memory integrity (VBS) off — is opt-in with -Aggressive, explained, and asked about again before it happens.

The fear

"What's it installing?"

A tray icon, an updater, a service of its own.

Nothing

The script runs from memory and exits. Nothing of it stays running, nothing starts with Windows, nothing checks for updates. What stays is a folder of text: the report, the checklist, the backups and the undo. The one exception, said plainly: a task that runs once at your next sign-in, writes the after-restart process count to that folder, and deletes itself. Skip it with -NoAfterCount.
