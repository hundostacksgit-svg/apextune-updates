## The short version

> **New Jersey, verified 16 September 2026.**
> - **The only equivalency exam New Jersey accepts is the GED.** HiSET ended in NJ on 31 May 2023; TASC ended in 2021. Do not buy HiSET materials or vouchers for New Jersey.
> - **Cost: $36 per subject, $144 for all four** — the same price at a test centre or online. Corroborated by Atlantic County's own `.gov` page, which states "The cost is $36.00 per subject."
> - **You must be a New Jersey resident.** Age 18+ books freely; 16- and 17-year-olds can test but need a signed Certificate of Consent (details below).
> - **Passing is 145 on each of the four subjects.** No averaging — 200 in Math does not rescue a 140 in Science.
> - **Book at `ged.com`.** NJ testing is delivered through Pearson VUE. A test centre is the better choice for most people: same price, cheaper retakes, more attempts before a lockout, and no practice-test prerequisite.
> - **Bring an unexpired NJ driver's licence or permit** — it proves both identity and residency in one document. No NJ licence means two documents instead (one photo ID plus one proof of NJ address).
> - **You get a New Jersey State-Issued High School Diploma**, mailed by the NJ Department of Education. Budget roughly four to six weeks from passing your fourth subject.
> - **Before you pay $144, make one phone call** to your county One-Stop Career Center and ask about fee assistance. Atlantic County states in writing that candidates who cannot afford the test may be eligible for help.

## Can you test in New Jersey

Three conditions, all mandatory. They come from state regulation, not from the test vendor.

`N.J.A.C. 6A:20-1.3`, "Eligibility requirements to earn a State-issued high school diploma":

> To participate in programs that lead to a State-issued high school diploma, a person shall be a New Jersey resident at least 16 years of age and no longer enrolled in school and shall have not yet obtained a high school diploma in New Jersey or any other state.

So: **New Jersey resident**, **16 or older**, **not currently enrolled in school**, and **no existing high school diploma or equivalency credential** from any state.

### The 16 versus 18 confusion — both numbers are correct

You will see New Jersey test centres and county workforce pages say flatly "participants must be at least 18 years or older." You will also see the regulation say 16. Neither is wrong; they describe different things.

- **18 and over:** the default. You create a `ged.com` account and book. There is no state form, no prep-course requirement, no orientation, no enrolment in adult education.
- **16 and 17:** eligible under the regulation, but your `ged.com` account carries an age restriction that a person at the test centre has to lift. That is what the consent form does.

### The paperwork for 16- and 17-year-olds

The form is the **Certificate of Consent to Participate**. One form, no separate "age waiver" process.

**Who may sign it.** This is broader than most people assume, and it matters. Per `N.J.A.C. 6A:20-1.3`, for a 16- or 17-year-old residing with a parent or guardian, the Certificate of Consent may be signed by any of:

- a natural or resource family parent (a resource family parent is a foster parent)
- a guardian
- a probation or parole officer
- a State rehabilitation counsellor
- a judge

A court order is one accepted route among several — not the default. This is the provision that makes the pathway workable for young people in foster care, on probation, or estranged from their parents.

**What the form certifies.** That you are at least 16, not currently enrolled in a public or private high school, and have not graduated from an accredited high school in the United States or Canada.

**How it gets executed — two routes:**

1. **Parent or legal guardian comes with you** to the test centre with the completed form.
2. **Parent cannot come:** the form must be signed by the parent **before a New Jersey notary**. You bring the notarised form to the **Chief Examiner** at the test centre, who verifies it and releases the age restriction on your account.

If a legal guardian rather than a parent signs, bring a copy of the court document awarding guardianship.

**One extra trap for minors testing online:** at least one source describes a parent or guardian having to be present at the pre-test check-in to authorise the minor being recorded, with the session revoked if they are absent. Whether that is an NJ rule or a general GED rule for minors, we could not pin down. If you are 16 or 17 and considering the at-home test, confirm this with your `ged.com` portal before booking — or simply test in person, where the rule is clear.

**The form:** `https://www.ged.com/content/dam/websites/ged/policies/new-jersey-certificate-consent.pdf` (the version we could identify is marked "Updated November 2021"). NJDOE also hosts a copy at `https://www.nj.gov/education/adulted/pathways/forms/ConsentParticipate.pdf`.

**Not confirmed:** one source says the signed consent must also be provided to the minor's current school district. We could not establish whether that is a statewide NJDOE requirement or a practice at particular centres. Ask your test centre's chief examiner.

### Residency

You must be a New Jersey resident, and you must prove it on test day. This is the single most common reason NJ candidates get turned away, and it is stricter than most states.

**The one-document route.** An unexpired New Jersey driver's licence or permit. The GED New Jersey policy page states this "can be used to satisfy both the photo ID requirement AND the Residency Requirement." Bring this if you have it and you are done.

**The two-document route**, if you do not:

| Group | What counts |
|---|---|
| **Photo ID** | Driver permit, passport, voter registration, school or college ID, employee ID, or other photo identification issued by a public entity or private third party. Must show name, signature, photo and date of birth. |
| **Proof of NJ residency** | Property tax bills, deeds, contracts of sale, leases, mortgages, **signed letters from landlords**, other evidence of ownership or tenancy; financial account information, utility bills, delivery receipts; court orders, State agency agreements and other evidence of court or agency placement; receipts, bills, cancelled cheques, insurance documents. |

Two things worth knowing. The **signed landlord letter** is an accepted residency document — a real lifeline if nothing is in your name. And the court-order and State-agency-placement options exist specifically for people whose housing is arranged by an agency.

**Not confirmed:** whether a non-driver **New Jersey State ID card** alone satisfies both requirements the way a licence or permit does. The policy wording we could retrieve names the licence and the permit. If that is your only document, ring your test centre before you travel, or bring a second residency document as backup.

**Residency also governs the online test.** If you sit New Jersey's online GED without being a New Jersey resident, you will not receive a diploma even if you pass all four subjects.

## New Jersey gives you a choice

New Jersey runs three routes to a high school credential for adults. Only one of them is an exam.

| Route | What it is | Age | Cost | Time | What you get |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GED** | Four computer-based subject tests: Reasoning Through Language Arts, Mathematical Reasoning, Science, Social Studies. Pass = 145 each. | 16+ (consent form under 18) | **$36 per subject, $144 total** | Days to weeks — you set the pace | **State-Issued High School Diploma** from NJDOE |
| **HiSET** | **Not available in New Jersey.** Discontinued 31 May 2023. | — | — | — | — |
| **TASC** | **Not available.** Retired nationally; NJ dropped it in 2021. | — | — | — | — |
| **Thirty College Credit Program (TCCP)** | Earn 30 college credits at a county college in a required distribution. No exam at all. | 16+, NJ resident, not enrolled in school, no existing diploma | **No charge for the NJDOE service.** You pay college tuition. | Semesters, unless you already hold the credits | **State-Issued High School Diploma** plus a diploma verification letter |
| **Adult High School Program (AHSP)** | Classroom coursework at a district adult high school. | 16+ | Set by the provider, not the State | Months | A **locally issued, State-endorsed** diploma — issued by the district board of education, not the Commissioner |

### If you already hold college credits, stop and read this

The **Thirty College Credit Program** is free and you may already have qualified without knowing it. It suits anyone who took college courses without finishing high school — homeschoolers, former dual-enrollment students, adults who took county college classes as non-matriculated students.

The credit distribution, per the NJDOE application, is:

- **3 credits each** in English language arts, mathematics (including statistics and probability), science, and social studies — 12 credits
- **6 credits** in any combination of visual and performing arts, comprehensive health and physical education, world language, technology, and 21st century life and careers
- **12 remaining credits** in any subject area
- **No remedial college courses count**, and the minimum average grade in each class is a **C (2.0)**

Official transcripts must be mailed to NJDOE directly from the college — unofficial copies are rejected.

> **A note on a discrepancy the researchers flagged.** The underlying regulation `N.J.A.C. 6A:8-5.2` is worded differently, describing a minimum of 15 credits with at least 3 each across five categories. The NJDOE application's distribution above is the one the Department actually administers and the one reproduced by county colleges. If you are choosing courses specifically to qualify, get the distribution confirmed in writing at **(609) 376-3883** before you enrol and pay tuition.

### Foreign diploma holders: there is no conversion route

New Jersey does not convert a foreign high school diploma into a New Jersey diploma. The sequence NJDOE points people to is:

1. Have the foreign transcript translated and evaluated by a credential evaluation service. NJDOE's FAQ names **World Education Services (WES)**; other New Jersey agencies accept any evaluator that is a member of **NACES** or **AICE**. These are private paid services and the State does not set the price.
2. If your college or employer accepts the evaluation, you are finished. You do not need a New Jersey diploma.
3. If they insist on a US credential, take the GED (NJDOE describes this as the quickest and most cost-effective option) or bring the evaluated transcript to a New Jersey adult high school for credit evaluation toward a local diploma.

**Not confirmed:** whether WES specifically is required or merely named as an example. Check `https://www.nj.gov/education/adulted/resources/faqs/`.

### The recommendation, if you want this done this week

**Take the GED, at a test centre, in person.** Reasoning:

- It is the only exam New Jersey accepts, so there is no comparison shopping to do.
- It is the only route that can finish inside a week. TCCP needs college credits you either have or do not; AHSP needs a term of coursework.
- In person costs exactly the same as at home — $36 a subject either way — but avoids the online route's mandatory practice test, its extra $26 to $32, and its 60-day timing window.
- If you fail a subject, an in-centre retake is **$10**. An online retake is the full **$36**.
- In centre you get three attempts at a subject before a 60-day lockout. Online you get two.

The one reason to choose online: you cannot travel, or you need an appointment outside business hours.

**Before you book, two checks worth ten minutes.** If you hold 30 or more college credits, apply to TCCP instead — it is free. And if you tested in New Jersey between 2014 and 2020 and scored between 145 and 149 on every subject, call NJDOE: New Jersey lowered its qualifying score from 150 to 145 and applied the change to earlier test takers, so you may already hold the diploma.

| Item | New Jersey price |
|---|---|
| Per subject, test centre | **$36.00** |
| Per subject, online proctored | **$36.00** — identical |
| All four subjects | **$144.00** |
| Discounted retake, test centre | **$10.00** per subject |
| Retake, online proctored | **$36.00** — no discount is offered online |
| GED Ready practice test | **$7.99** per subject, or **$25.99** for the four-subject bundle |

The $36 is corroborated by a New Jersey county government page — Atlantic County Workforce Development states "The cost is $36.00 per subject" — which is stronger evidence than the prep-industry sites that also carry it. Still confirm the figure in your `ged.com` cart before you click pay; that total is the only number that binds.

### How the retake discount works

The $36 is two fees stacked: a **$26 GED Testing Service fee** plus a **$10 New Jersey test-centre fee**. On a discounted retake, GED Testing Service waives its $26 and you pay the $10 that remains. That arithmetic is why the $10 figure is credible.

The entitlement: **one discounted retake for every subject you buy at full price and do not pass**, usable within **365 days** of the full-price attempt. The one-full-price-to-one-discount cycle then repeats until you pass.

### Retake waiting periods

- **Test centre:** no wait after the first or second failure on a subject. After **three attempts** at a subject, you wait **60 days**.
- **Online proctored:** one retake, then a **60-day wait**. Two attempts at a subject online and you are locked out of that subject online for 60 days.

If a subject feels marginal, that asymmetry alone argues for the test centre.

### GED Ready

GED Ready is the official practice test. It is **optional if you test at a centre** and **mandatory if you test online** — see the next section.

At **$7.99 a subject or $25.99 for all four**, buy the bundle if you need more than three. Each purchase is a single sitting; you cannot re-sit a GED Ready test on the same purchase.

> Prices for GED Ready have shifted in recent years and older figures ($6.99 each, $22.99 bundle) still circulate on prep sites. Budget **$26 to $32** for four and read the live cart. The page that settles it is `https://www.ged.com/faqs/ged-ready.html`.

### Realistic totals

- **Test centre, pass everything first time:** **$144**
- **Online, which forces four GED Ready passes first:** **$144 + $25.99 = about $170**
- Each failed subject retaken in centre: **+$10**. Retaken online: **+$36**.

### How New Jersey compares nationally

Most states sit in a **$30 to $40 per subject** band, roughly $120 to $160 for all four. New Jersey's $36 is mid-range to slightly cheap, and unusually, NJ charges the **same** for online as in centre — several states charge a premium for the at-home test. A handful of states (Connecticut, New York and West Virginia among them) subsidise the exam entirely. New Jersey does not have a comparable statewide subsidy that we could confirm, but county-level help does exist — see **Money help**.

## Booking it this week

### The click path

1. Create an account at **`ged.com`**. New Jersey GED testing is delivered through **Pearson VUE**, but you register and pay through `ged.com`, not Pearson's own site.
2. Confirm your state is set to New Jersey. Residency drives which policies your account gets.
3. Choose **test centre** or **online proctored**.
4. Search test centres by ZIP code with a wide radius, pick a date, and pay.
5. Book **one subject first**, not all four. See the checklist for why.
6. If you are 16 or 17, get the Certificate of Consent sorted before your appointment.

Accommodations for a disability, learning difference or medical condition are requested through **GED Testing Service before you schedule**. Decisions are individual and take time, so start that first if it applies to you. NJDOE also publishes guidance at `https://nj.gov/education/adulted/resources/acc`.

### In person or at home, for New Jersey specifically

**At home requires a practice test first.** For each subject you want to sit online you must take the official **GED Ready** test for that subject, score in the **green "Likely to Pass"** band, and then **schedule the real exam within 60 days** of that green score. A green score older than 60 days does not qualify you. There is no equivalent requirement at a test centre — you can book and sit cold.

**At home requires New Jersey residency**, enforced at the credential stage: pass all four as a non-resident and no diploma is issued.

**At home requires specific equipment.** From Pearson VUE's OnVUE requirements:

- A desktop or laptop computer. **Tablets and phones cannot deliver the exam.**
- A webcam at eye level showing head and shoulders. **A phone cannot be used as the webcam.**
- **Built-in or wired** speakers, and wired headphones if any. No Bluetooth.
- **A single monitor.** Multiple displays are not allowed, and touch screens are not allowed.
- A reliable connection, wired Ethernet preferred over Wi-Fi. **No VPN or proxy** — this alone disqualifies many work-managed laptops.

**At home requires a room you control.** Four walls, a closed door, a 360-degree room scan at check-in, **nobody else entering for any reason**, and you may not leave the webcam's view at any point. Check-in starts **30 minutes before** your appointment. Breaking the rules gets the exam revoked, the fee forfeited, and can bar you from testing. Run Pearson VUE's system test on the actual machine in the actual room before you book anything.

**The honest recommendation:** for someone booking this week, the test centre wins on every axis except travel — same price, no practice-test gate, an extra attempt before lockout, $10 retakes instead of $36, and none of the equipment failure modes that cost you the fee outright.

### Realistic lead time

**Online:** Pearson VUE staffs online proctors around the clock, so appointments exist 24/7. The constraint is not the calendar, it is the green GED Ready score you must earn first. The genuine one-week online path is: buy GED Ready today, score green today, book, sit. That works if you score green first time. If you do not, the route collapses and you are better off at a centre.

**Test centre:** plausible within a week but not guaranteed. Centres run business hours with finite seats; one New Jersey centre publishes slots only on Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday mornings and afternoons. County college centres thin out around semester starts and holidays.

**Not confirmed:** we could not find published appointment lead times for New Jersey centres, and they genuinely vary by site and season. The `ged.com` scheduler is the only answer. Search your ZIP with a wide radius before committing to a plan — someone willing to drive 45 minutes will usually find a seat inside a week; someone insisting on the nearest centre may not.

## What New Jersey actually issues

### The name of the credential

> **New Jersey State-Issued High School Diploma**

Not a "GED certificate." New Jersey does not issue a GED certificate at all. The GED is the assessment; the diploma is the credential, and the State issues it. `N.J.A.C. 6A:8-5.2` defines it as a high school diploma provided by the Department of Education to persons 16 or older, no longer enrolled in school, documenting attainment of academic skills and knowledge equivalent to a high school education.

There is a legal distinction worth knowing:

- **State-issued** diploma — awarded by the **Commissioner of Education**. This is what the GED route and the TCCP route produce.
- **State-endorsed** diploma — issued by a **district board of education**. This is what the adult high school route produces.

Both are high school diplomas. Colleges and employers treat them as equivalent to a traditional diploma, and the state-issued diploma is accepted for admission at New Jersey county colleges.

### Who issues it

> **New Jersey Department of Education**
> Office of Career Readiness, Adult Education
> PO Box 500, Trenton, NJ 08625-0500
> **(609) 376-3883** · **adulted@doe.nj.gov**

Use that number and that address. An older number, **(609) 376-3500**, and an older mailbox, `adultedinfo@doe.nj.gov`, circulate on third-party and library pages; `-3883` and `adulted@doe.nj.gov` are the pair that appear on NJDOE's current diploma and transcript pages.

### How the diploma and transcript arrive

Pass all four subjects at 145 or above and NJDOE mails you the diploma and an official transcript of your test results. TCCP applicants also get a diploma verification letter. You do not apply — it is automatic once the scores reach the State.

**Timing.** NJDOE's stated processing is **5 to 7 business days** for results to reach the State from the test vendor, then **3 to 5 business days** for the State to process and mail. Postal delivery can add up to another two weeks. **Plan on four to six weeks** from your fourth passing subject. If a job offer or an enrolment deadline depends on it, ring (609) 376-3883 rather than guessing.

### The access code system — this is how everything afterwards works

New Jersey does not mail transcripts or verifications on request. It runs a self-service portal, and the key to it is a code printed on your documents.

- Every NJ state-issued diploma and transcript since **May 2007** has an **access code printed at the bottom**. Anyone who tested on an approved test after **1 January 2014** is issued one.
- **Access code and verification code are different and not interchangeable.** They go in different fields on the portal. Enter your access code in the field marked *Access Code*, not the one marked *Verification Code*.
- Rule of thumb from NJDOE: **the transcript is for school or training; the diploma verification is for employment.**

**The portal:** `https://homeroom4.doe.state.nj.us/GED2016/PgPF.jsp` — "Diploma Verification / Print Duplicate Diploma." With your code you can download, save and print your official transcript and an official duplicate copy of your diploma.

**There is no "send to institution" button.** You print the PDF and send it yourself, or you give an employer your verification code so they can confirm the credential directly on the State's system.

### Replacements

New Jersey issues **only one original diploma** and does not re-print and re-mail a second original. That is what "duplicate diplomas are not issued in New Jersey" means, and it is why that phrase appears alongside instructions for getting a duplicate — the two are not in conflict. What you can do, after **six months of non-receipt** or if yours is lost or damaged, is request your access code and **print an official duplicate copy yourself** from the portal.

**Lost your access code?** Two routes, both by post: complete the **Access Code Request Form** (`https://www.nj.gov/education/adulted/verification/Access%20code%20request%20form.pdf`) or send a signed letter, to the Trenton PO Box 500 address above.

**Name wrong or changed?** Use the **Correction to Adult Education Record Request Form** (`https://www.nj.gov/education/adulted/verification/Change%20of%20Record%20Form.pdf`). It has a field for "name at time of test if different," which is how a married or changed name is reconciled.

**Not confirmed: fees.** No NJDOE page we could reach states a fee for an access code, a transcript print, a diploma verification or a duplicate print, and the self-service design strongly implies there is none. But no page affirmatively says "free" either. Confirm at `https://www.nj.gov/education/adulted/verification/` before paying anyone anything.

> **Do not pay a third party for a New Jersey transcript.** Some aggregator pages claim `diplomasender.com` is New Jersey's official transcript provider and quote a fee. We found no NJDOE source supporting that, and NJDOE's own pages describe the State-run portal above. Diploma Sender's documented state contracts are elsewhere. The "$15 GED transcript" figure that circulates is another vendor's price in states it serves, not a New Jersey price. Check the free NJDOE portal first.

### What your scores buy beyond the diploma

The GED score bands are national:

| Score per subject | Band | What it may mean |
|---|---|---|
| 145–164 | Passing | Earns the NJ State-Issued High School Diploma |
| 165–174 | College Ready | May waive college placement testing or developmental coursework |
| 175–200 | College Ready + Credit | May earn college credit, up to 10 credits |

The waiver is real in New Jersey but **college by college, not statewide**. A confirmed example: **Hudson County Community College** exempts you from mathematics placement testing if you took the GED **within the past 5 years** and scored **165 or above in Mathematical Reasoning**, with a matching rule for English and Reasoning Through Language Arts — but you must send documentation to admissions; it is not automatic. Note that five-year recency limit. By contrast, **County College of Morris** directs state-issued-diploma entrants to take the placement test, with no documented automatic GED waiver.

**Not confirmed:** we could not find a single New Jersey county college publishing an explicit policy awarding actual college credit for GED scores of 175 or above. The national policy says the decision rests entirely with each institution. **Do not pay for extra attempts chasing 175 on the assumption it converts into New Jersey college credit.** Ring the specific college's registrar or prior-learning office and ask before you spend.

One more practical note: New Jersey four-year institutions frequently want more than the diploma. Rutgers requires official GED results **plus transcripts of any and all high school work attempted**, and Montclair State and Kean also ask for secondary school records. Keep whatever partial high school transcript you have.

Prep in New Jersey is routinely free even though the test is not. There is no reason to pay for a prep course before checking these.

- **Bergen One-Stop Career Center** runs high school equivalency preparation.

### Literacy New Jersey

Statewide, free, organised county by county. Trained volunteer tutors work with adults in small groups on reading, writing, maths and English, usually meeting at a local library. Start at `https://literacynj.org/` and find your county programme page. Also **Project Literacy of Bergen County**.

### New Jersey Youth Corps — if you are 16 to 25

Funded by the NJ Department of Labor and Workforce Development, for out-of-school youth aged 16 to 25 who have not completed high school. It combines academic instruction and HSE exam preparation with employability and life-skills training, community service, counselling — plus a **stipend of $100 per week** based on full-week attendance, and transport and childcare support.

Roughly **11 sites across 10 counties**, including Camden, Elizabeth, Jersey City, New Brunswick, Newark, Paterson, Phillipsburg, Pleasantville, Trenton and Vineland. Eligibility is geographic, so check which site serves your address.

- **`mycareer.nj.gov`** — NJDOL's training explorer. Searchable, and it lists cost per programme; several show **$0**.
- **NJ 2-1-1** (`nj211.org`) — human services search including HSE programmes.
- **SkillUp New Jersey** — thousands of free online courses for NJ workers. General skills, not GED-specific.

## Money help

The fee you are trying to cover is **$144** for four subjects at $36 each.

### The call to make before you pay

**Do not book and pay first.** Ring your county One-Stop Career Center and ask about test-fee assistance. The clearest statement in writing comes from a county government page:

> "Exam candidates who cannot afford the cost of the test may be eligible for financial assistance from the One Stop Career Center."
> — Atlantic County Workforce Development

That is a county `.gov` page, which is the strongest evidence of fee assistance we could find anywhere in New Jersey.

### Routes that are documented

| Route | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **County One-Stop Career Center assistance** | The test fee, for candidates who cannot afford it. Explicitly documented for Atlantic County; other counties operate similar discretion under WIOA. |
| **Literacy New Jersey** | Free classes, and explicitly "assists with scheduling and paying for GED exams." |
| **NJ Youth Corps (16–25)** | Free programme plus a $100 weekly attendance stipend, transport and childcare. |
| **WIOA Title I funds via local Workforce Development Boards** | Basic-skills services and co-enrolment with Title II adult education. |
| **WIOA Title II adult education providers** | Free instruction statewide; many also handle test logistics. |
| **Monmouth County Youth GED Program** | GED prep, testing and support for youth 16–24 certified by the Monmouth County One-Stop. Listed on `mycareer.nj.gov` at a total cost of $0. |

**Not confirmed: a statewide New Jersey voucher or fee waiver.** We could not find one. Fee help in New Jersey appears to be county-by-county and discretionary, run through One-Stops and WIOA-funded providers. A reader outside Atlantic or Monmouth must ask their own county — the absence of a published page is not the absence of the benefit. Ignore prep-industry pages that vaguely claim "some organizations provide vouchers" without naming one.

### The larger money, after you pass

Once you hold the state-issued diploma, the **Community College Opportunity Grant (CCOG)** can make a county college associate degree tuition-free:

- **Adjusted gross income $0 to $65,000:** CCOG covers remaining tuition (up to 18 credits a term) and approved education fees after other grants and scholarships are applied.
- **AGI $65,001 to $80,000:** reduced tuition, after up to 50% of the maximum CCOG award at your college is applied.
- Also required: New Jersey residency, a **FAFSA or NJ Alternative Financial Aid Application filed each year by the State deadline**, enrolment in **at least 6 credits per semester**, no prior college degree, and satisfactory academic progress.

Income bands and deadlines change annually. Verify the current year's thresholds at `https://www.hesaa.org/pages/ccog.aspx`.

This is also the cheapest route to the 30 credits behind the TCCP pathway, and to the 15 college credits that change how a GED holder is tiered for military enlistment — though if the military is your goal, confirm current tier rules with a recruiter rather than any guide, because the services adjust them with recruiting conditions.

## The NJ checklist

From no account to sitting your first subject, for someone who wants to test within seven days.

1. **Today, 10 minutes — check whether you should be testing at all.** If you hold 30 or more college credits, apply to the Thirty College Credit Program instead; it costs nothing. If you tested in NJ between 2014 and 2020 and scored 145 to 149 on all four subjects, ring **(609) 376-3883** — you may already have earned the diploma under the lowered qualifying score.
2. **Today, 15 minutes — make the money call.** Ring your county One-Stop Career Center and ask whether the $144 test fee can be covered for you, and ring Literacy New Jersey's county office, which helps with both scheduling and paying. Ten minutes on the phone can be worth the whole fee. Do this before you pay, not after.
3. **Today, 5 minutes — assemble your documents.** Best case: an unexpired New Jersey driver's licence or permit, which covers identity and residency in one. Otherwise, one photo ID showing name, signature, photo and date of birth, **plus** one proof of NJ address — utility bill, lease, tax bill, or a signed letter from your landlord. Originals, not photocopies. Your name must match your `ged.com` registration exactly.
4. **Today, if you are 16 or 17 — start the consent form.** Download the Certificate of Consent to Participate. Either arrange for a parent or guardian to come with you to the centre, or get their signature **notarised by a New Jersey notary** and plan to hand it to the Chief Examiner. If a guardian signs, bring the guardianship court document. This is the step most likely to slip a week, so start it first.
5. **Today, 20 minutes — create your `ged.com` account** and set your state to New Jersey.
6. **Today, 10 minutes — open the scheduler and look at the actual calendar.** Search your ZIP with a wide radius. The available dates decide whether a seven-day plan is real. If nothing is within reach, widen the radius before you consider the online route.
7. **Today — choose in person unless you cannot travel.** Same $36, no practice-test gate, three attempts before a lockout instead of two, and $10 retakes instead of $36. If you do choose online: buy GED Ready for your first subject ($7.99, or $25.99 for all four), sit it, and you need a **green** score, after which you must schedule the real exam within 60 days. Run Pearson VUE's system test on the actual computer in the actual room before you book.
8. **Day 1 to 2 — book one subject, not four.** Book the subject you are strongest in. Passing it tells you the process works and your documents were accepted; failing it costs $36 rather than $144 of scheduling you have to rearrange. Pay attention to the price on the checkout screen — it should read $36 for one subject. If it does not, stop and ring the centre.
9. **Day 2 to 6 — prepare for free.** Free GED prep exists in every county: your One-Stop, your county college's adult basic skills programme, your public library (Newark Public Library runs free classes Tuesday, Wednesday and Thursday evenings), or a Literacy New Jersey volunteer tutor. Search the WIOA Title II directory for your county.
10. **The night before — pack.** Photo ID and residency document, originals. Consent form if you are 16 or 17. Your own TI-30XS MultiView calculator if you have one and are sitting Maths at a centre — it is the only permitted physical calculator, and an on-screen version is provided regardless. Leave behind, because they are prohibited in the room: phone, watch, hat, bag, coat, wallet, keys and key fobs, books and notes.
11. **Test day — arrive at least 15 minutes early.** More than 15 minutes late and you may be refused entry and lose the fee. Unscheduled breaks are not allowed; leaving the room outside a scheduled break means your test is not scored. If you are testing at home, begin check-in **30 minutes** before your appointment.
12. **After — repeat for the remaining three subjects.** 145 or better on each, no averaging. If you fail one, an in-centre retake is $10 within 365 days and there is no waiting period for the first two retakes.
13. **After the fourth pass — wait, then chase.** Expect four to six weeks for the diploma and transcript by post (5 to 7 business days for scores to reach the State, 3 to 5 to process and mail, then postal time). If nothing arrives, ring **(609) 376-3883**. When it does arrive, **photograph the access code printed at the bottom** and store it somewhere permanent — that code is how you print replacements and how employers verify you, forever.
