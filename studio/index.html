The part nobody else has

Describe the edit. It does the edit.

Drop your clips in, type what you're after, and OmniDx Studio plans the whole thing — which clip goes where, how long each one holds, where the cuts land against the music, what the captions say and how they look. Say "anime edit, 30 seconds, starts slow and goes crazy" and it builds the montage section by section — the drop with its speed ramps and impact frames, the breather, the title, the music fitted to the end. No music? It makes a beat in the right style so every cut lands. Every step lands on the timeline as a normal edit you can drag, trim or undo. Nothing is locked, nothing is a black box.

The AI editor is a paid feature — it starts at Creator, $19.99 once. Try the planner below for free, right here, before you decide. Everything that runs on your own device stays free forever, including the one-tap edit styles, which build a whole edit with no AI at all.

The editor

Everything you'd expect. Laid out where you expect it.

If you've used DaVinci Resolve, Premiere or CapCut, you already know where things are. Media on the left, viewer in the middle, inspector on the right, timeline underneath, pages across the bottom. We didn't reinvent the layout — we removed the parts that make people quit.

🎬

Multitrack timeline

Unlimited video and audio tracks, ripple and roll trims, razor, slip, snapping you can switch off, markers, and a magnetic mode for when you want one.

🎨

Colour that means something

Exposure, contrast, temperature, tint, highlights, shadows, curves and vignette — plus 118 looks. Scopes for the people who want them, hidden for the people who don't.

💬

Captions that fit the platform

Auto-timed captions in TikTok, YouTube and clean broadcast styles, inside the safe zones for each app, editable word by word.

🔊

Audio that stays in sync

Waveforms, volume curves, fades, ducking under voice, beat detection, and one-click silence removal. Sample-accurate, always.

⚡

Speed and keyframes

Speed ramps with real easing curves, freeze frames, reverse, and keyframes on every transform and colour value with bezier handles.

📐

Every aspect ratio

9:16, 1:1, 4:5, 16:9 and 2.39:1 with live safe-zone overlays showing exactly where TikTok's buttons will cover your text.

🧩

Transitions worth using

Dissolve, dip to black or white, slide, whip pan, zoom punch, glitch and film burn. Draggable duration, no dialog box.

🎯

Motion tracking

Draw a box round a face, a phone, a number plate. It follows it through the shot and you pin a blur, a sticker or a title to it. Runs on your device, no model to download.

⟿

Real motion blur

Sampled from the actual movement — a push, a shake, a speed ramp all blur in the right direction. Not a directional smear you dial in by hand.

🎨

Three-way colour wheels

Lift, gamma and gain, running as a GPU filter rather than a per-pixel loop — so grading stays real-time on a laptop.

🎧

Studio audio repair

Spectral noise reduction, mains-hum removal, click repair and automatic levelling. The four things people open a DAW for, done here, offline.

🎞

Professional codecs

H.264 and H.265 encoded frame by frame — faster than real time, and the tab can go in the background. Apple ProRes and Avid DNxHR from the desktop app, with no extra licence to buy.

📦

Projects that actually move

A bundle carries your footage with the edit, so a project opens intact on your phone, a friend's laptop or a desktop install. It is also just a ZIP — your files are never locked in.

⚡

Proxies that make themselves

Editing 4K on an old laptop stops stuttering. A 540p copy is used while you work and never while you export — so a proxy can't end up in your finished video.

📱

Awkward files just open

Variable-frame-rate phone video and screen recordings work without converting anything first. Everything is driven by timestamps, so audio can't drift out over ten minutes.

🗂

Projects you can move

A project is one readable JSON file. Copy it to another machine, email it, put it in git. No database, no library, no "project not found".

⌘

Command palette

Ctrl+K finds any tool, effect or setting by name. Nothing is buried three menus deep.

⭐

290 presets, one tap each

A grade, a stack of effects with their settings tuned to each other, a reframe and an audio strip — saved together. Forty-nine groups from film stocks to datamosh, plus your own saved off any clip you got right.

🎥

Multicam

Drop in every angle and they sync themselves off their own audio. Then watch them side by side and cut live with the number keys, exactly as it played.

🪄

176 edit styles

Anime, phonk, trailer, wedding, horror, kaleidoscope. A style goes on top of the cut you already made — try thirty without losing an hour of work.

📲

Every platform from one edit

One press renders vertical, landscape, square and portrait from the same cut. Each is reframed on a copy — a tracked subject stays mid-frame, the picture never shows an edge — and named by shape. Your project keeps its own.

🎯

A project that reads the clip

Drop footage on the start screen and the project takes its frame size and its measured frame rate — 29.97 comes out as 29.97, not a guess — and the clips land on the timeline. Seven one-press setups for everything else, and the last one used is offered next time.

🔑

An account that moves with you

Accounts live on the device that made them. A move code — made with your password, unreadable without it — carries the account and anything you have bought to another device in one paste. Nothing goes through a server.

Under the hood

The things other editors charge extra for.

Delivery codecs behind a separate licence. Audio repair that means buying a second program. Proxies that don't work. Project files that won't open on the other machine. These are the reasons people give up on an editor — so they are the ones we built first.

🎞️

ProRes & H.265, no extra licence

Apple ProRes 422 / 4444 and Avid DNxHR on the desktop app, H.265/HEVC everywhere the hardware can do it. Included in Studio, not sold separately, not a plug-in.

🎛️

Studio audio repair

Broadband noise reduction, mains hum removal, click repair and automatic vocal levelling — measured, not guessed. In testing, 60 Hz hum dropped 12.6× with the voice untouched. No second app, no round trip to a DAW.

🌊

Creative audio filters

Concert hall, tape echo, ping-pong, flanger, phaser, rotary speaker, tube warmth, cassette, walkie-talkie, movie trailer, monster, sidechain pump, tape stop, rain, shimmer — 148 of them in twelve groups, live while you scrub and rendered into the export. 136 are free.

⚡

Proxies that just happen

Anything too heavy for your machine gets a low-resolution stand-in built quietly in the background. You edit on the proxy, you export from the original, and you never have to think about which one you're looking at.

📥

Opens what your phone records

Variable-frame-rate phone video and screen recordings — the two formats that quietly desync audio in other editors — are measured on import and laid down on a fixed timebase. No manual conversion step, ever.

📦

One project file, every device

An .omnidxpkg holds the timeline, the effects and the footage in one real ZIP. Start on the laptop, finish on the phone, nothing lost — and if we vanish tomorrow you can still open it, because the format is documented.

Why this exists

We read the complaints. Then we fixed them.

Every one of these is something people say, loudly and often, about the editor they currently use. They're the reason OmniDx Studio is built the way it is.

Premiere Pro

"It crashed and I lost four hours"

Autosave that's off by default, set to 20 minutes, and writes to a folder you can't find.

Autosave every 5 seconds, on by default

Plus a version snapshot on every significant change and a crash-recovery prompt the next time you open. You cannot lose more than five seconds of work.

Premiere Pro

"Media offline. Where did my files go?"

Move one folder and the project is a wall of red.

Media is remembered by content, not by path

Files are fingerprinted. Move or rename them and the project relinks itself; if it truly can't find one, it asks for that single file instead of breaking the timeline.

CapCut

"Another feature went Pro overnight"

Free features quietly move behind a subscription, and the watermark comes back.

The free tier is fixed in writing

The whole editor, no watermark, no export limit, forever. What's paid is listed on the pricing page and doesn't move. Buy once at $39.99 and it's yours — no renewal.

DaVinci Resolve

"I opened it once and closed it forever"

Seven pages, a thousand controls, no idea where to start.

Three skill levels, switchable any time

Beginner shows nine controls. Professional shows all of them, at finishing density. Intermediate sits in between. You move up when you're ready, not when the software decides.

DaVinci Resolve

"Playback stutters unless I build proxies"

Editing 4K on a normal laptop means a transcoding chore first.

Proxies build themselves, quietly

Preview resolution drops automatically while you scrub and snaps back when you stop. Export always uses the full-quality original.

A failed export still gives you a file

Encoded footage is written continuously, so if something goes wrong you keep everything rendered up to that point as a playable file — and it names the timecode and the clip it stopped on, in plain English.

Grows with you

Three editors in one. You pick which one you get.

The same project, the same file, the same export. What changes is the density — how much machinery is on screen and how tightly it is packed. Switch level any time from the top bar. It takes effect instantly and never touches your edit.

Level 01 · Guided

Beginner

Nine buttons. A guided path from "import" to "posted". If you've only ever used your phone's camera roll, start here.

  • Big obvious tools, no panels to arrange
  • An interactive walkthrough you can skip
  • One-tap looks instead of colour sliders
  • Export presets named after the app you're posting to
  • The AI assistant front and centre
Level 02 · Editing

Intermediate

The full timeline and the real inspector at a working density — everything an editor uses daily, without the parts that need a colourist's vocabulary.

  • Multitrack timeline: ripple, roll, slip, razor
  • The inspector, back on screen: transform, crop, opacity, blend
  • Colour sliders and the whole look library
  • Audio mixing, ducking and all 148 audio filters
  • Motion tracking and markers
  • Every shortcut printed on the control it belongs to
Level 03 · Finishing

Professional

Finishing-suite density: smaller type, tighter rows, a taller timeline, tabular figures on every field — and a deliberately neutral chrome, because the image is the only thing on screen that should be saturated.

  • Curves, scopes, HSL qualifiers, unlimited LUT slots
  • Three-way colour wheels with per-clip lift, gamma and gain
  • Keyframes with bezier easing on every property
  • Speed ramping and time remapping
  • Frame-accurate timecode, not seconds, on every field
  • ProRes 422 / 4444, DNxHR and H.265 delivery
  • Custom keyboard maps and a command palette
  • Raw project JSON, editable in place

The greying-out is the point. Judging a grade next to bright blue furniture is your own eye lying to you — which is why every serious finishing application is grey, and why ours is too at the level where people are doing that work.

Nothing is ever deleted by moving down a level. Drop from Professional to Beginner and the curve you drew is still there, still applied, still in the file — it is just not on screen while you don't need it.
